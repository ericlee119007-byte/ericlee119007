Stacked 64-channel microfluidic multiplexer

Undergraduate Researcher · MEMS & Microfluidics Lab, University of Maryland · 2024–2026

A reusable multiplexer that sits outside the microfluidic chip it controls, so one multiplexer can drive many disposable chips. Instead of growing sideways, it stacks: four identical 16-plex control layers share eight control lines through vias, and a 4-plex routing layer on top sends pressure to only one of them. Any of 64 outputs can be selected with 12 control lines, for applications including T-cell probing and soft robotic actuation. I led the project.

  • CNC-micromilled 1.6 mm PMMA plates with channels on one side and ball-milled valve seats on the other, bonded to 20 µm PDMS membranes
  • Characterized valve flow vs. control pressure in a 2×2 design of experiments (membrane thickness × channel depth), with triplicate measurements, to set the final 250 µm seat / 20 µm membrane design
  • Developed CNC micromachining, SLA microprinting, soft lithography, and thermoplastic bonding processes and wrote their SOPs
  • Python control software with a grid UI for stepping through outputs, plus a Python pipeline that automates data acquisition, cleaning, and publication figures
  • A clamped interface to disposable PDMS chips, plus 16-plex and 64-plex demo chips cast from 3D-printed molds
First-author manuscript in preparation

EBUS-TBNA surgical training model

Senior capstone · UMD Bioengineering · 2025–2026

A high-fidelity training model for endobronchial ultrasound-guided needle aspiration at roughly 1/200th the cost of leading models. I led design, fabrication, and integration of the soft 3D-printed bronchial model. Early on we moved away from reconstructing anatomy from CT scans to a custom model dimensioned from the literature.

  • Characterized the mechanical and echogenic properties of ten hydrogel tissue analogs to match airway and lymph-node behavior under ultrasound, guiding material selection
  • Team point of contact for physician and faculty mentors: ran meetings, set agendas, and translated CAD and 3D printing decisions for a clinical audience

Organ-on-chip models and custom hardware

Microphysiological Systems Intern · Air Force Research Laboratory, 711th HPW · Summer 2025

I seeded and cultured stem cell–derived brain cells in organ-on-a-chip devices to build brain models for studying how oxygen levels affect brain health. I also designed and 3D printed custom parts that extend the Emulate system to hold a bioactive tooth model.

  • Brain-on-a-chip cultures run under controlled oxygen in a hypoxia workstation
  • Molds for casting bioactive hydroxyapatite/polycaprolactone (HA/PCL) teeth
  • A deposition tool that places the HA tooth into the chip, and custom lids that hold it in place
  • A microneedle array that creates pores in the chip's gel
